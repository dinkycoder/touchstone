# 6. Agents that pay: x402

**By the end, the reader can:**
- walk through an x402 payment from first request to final response
- explain what a facilitator checks, and what it can't know
- explain why a payment of a fraction of a cent works here and not on a card network
- point to where Touchstone sits in the flow

## Outline

1. **HTTP 402.** A status code reserved for "Payment Required" and left unused for decades.
2. **The x402 flow.** The client requests a resource; the server answers 402 with its payment terms; the
   client signs a payment and retries; the server has a facilitator verify and settle it; the server
   responds with proof of settlement. Header and scheme names get checked against the current spec
   version, not remembered.
3. **Facilitators.** What "verify" and "settle" each do. Coinbase's hosted facilitator and its built-in
   address screening. Other facilitators.
4. **The economics of tiny payments.** No card fees and no accounts; typical payment sizes (dated;
   Chapter 16).
5. **Where a trust check fits.** Before settlement: before the seller serves the request, or before the
   buyer pays. And Touchstone as an x402 endpoint itself, where every check is a paid call.
