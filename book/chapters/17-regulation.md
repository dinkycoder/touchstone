# 17. Regulation and the compliance clock

Education, not legal advice. Each section quotes or points to the primary text.

**By the end, the reader can:**
- explain what the GENIUS Act requires of stablecoin issuers regarding sanctions, and when
- explain why secondary-market screening creates demand for agent-aware tools
- list the components OFAC expects in a sanctions compliance program
- name the other regimes that constrain Touchstone's design

## Outline

1. **The GENIUS Act in brief.** Permitted payment stablecoin issuers, and what "effective sanctions
   compliance programs" means under the law.
2. **The implementing rule.** The FinCEN and OFAC proposal: its primary- and secondary-market
   obligations, its deadline, and its nod to AI-driven monitoring. Proposed and final text checked
   separately.
3. **What regulators expect from a program.** OFAC's framework: management commitment, risk assessment,
   internal controls, testing and auditing, training.
4. **What the industry told the regulator.** Themes from the public comment letters (H4).
5. **Other constraints on the design.** Why Touchstone doesn't score natural persons for credit in phase
   one (US consumer-reporting law), how the EU AI Act classifies credit scoring, and why evidence stays
   offchain.
