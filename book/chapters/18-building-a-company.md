# 18. Building a company on it

**By the end, the reader can:**
- name Touchstone's first buyers, and why they come before sellers and agents
- explain the per-check unit economics and what has to be true for them to work
- explain the main risks (timing, platform, regulatory) and how each is mitigated
- say what the capstone proves and what a pitch still needs

## Outline

1. **Who buys first.** Stablecoin issuers and facilitators facing the compliance deadline, then sellers,
   then agents.
2. **Pricing at the grain of the payment.** Why screening has to cost a small fraction of a payment that
   averages cents, and what that means for architecture (H5).
3. **The data advantage.** Every check, confirmed hit and dispute improves the entity graph.
4. **The credit path.** Why credit is phase two, and how Touchstone's data feeds it.
5. **Funding.** What Base Batches and the Base Ecosystem Fund say they look for, read at the source (H10).
6. **Risks and mitigations.**
   - timing: agent volume stays small
   - platform: Coinbase builds it in
   - regulatory: false positives harm legitimate operators; false negatives expose customers
7. **What the capstone proves, and what it can't.** A working, measured system on Sepolia versus the
   commitments a fund wants: design partners and a letter of intent.
